2. Former BLOCKER Closure

R-01 Immutable Versioning — PARTIALLY CLOSED

Closed for the objects R-01 named. §5.1 establishes the stable-identity / immutable-version split for Knowledge Units, Guardrails, Interpretation Rules, Construct definitions, and Rights decisions. §5.2 draws a coherent draft/post-draft immutability boundary with indefinite historical resolvability. §5.4 explicitly prohibits the v0.1 mutable integer version column as a sole mechanism. §5.5 separates version revision from conceptual supersession and requires scope-qualified partial supersession. §17.2 splits lineage (superseded, retired) from availability and approval, so supersession no longer destroys resolvability. §5.3 correctly makes version identity mandatory wherever an audit must reconstruct what was true, while permitting stable-identity references only for governed, auditable "current version" resolution. On the test's literal question — can a past runtime record resolve to the exact rule, guardrail, KU, construct definition, and rights state? — the answer is yes for those five.

Not closed on coverage. Two in-place mutation paths for governance-bearing semantic content survive, and both reproduce the original R-01 failure one hop further out:

1. Evidence anchors are not versioned. §9.2 gives rgkb.evidence_anchor a locator payload, span offsets, anchor_text_hash, and optionally retained excerpt text — none declared immutable, and anchors are absent from §5.1's list. The typed link from an immutable KU version (§9.3) is fixed, but its target can move. A corrected page range, re-extracted excerpt, or re-pointed manifestation silently changes what a past KU version appears to have been justified by. §23.1 terminates the traceability chain at the anchor, so this is directly on the audit path.
2. The localized governed text actually shown to a student is not version-bound in the runtime record. §18.5 asserts authoritative Georgian text is "versioned", but localized text appears in neither §5.1's list nor §5.3's reference rule; §23.1 requires only "localization identity" (not version identity) to be resolvable; and §22.2's runtime record enumerates rule, guardrail, KU, validation, rights, review, and model versions — but not the localized-text version. §23.3's own argument ("resolves to whatever that KU says now proves nothing about what was said to a student last term") applies with full force to the Georgian rendering, which is the artefact a minor actually read.

Secondary unversioned governed objects with the same exposure: construct_scale_mapping (§10.4 explicitly calls it a scientific claim that is evidence-linked and validation-governable), knowledge_unit_version_relation (§11.1 — its predicate is the governed fact), and cross_source_position (§13.2). This also collides with §12.3, which makes recording the exact subject VERSION reviewed mandatory: several validation-governable subjects have no version to record.

R-02 Runtime Decision Provenance — CLOSED

§9.1 states the two chains explicitly and gives each its own question ("why is this justified?" vs "what did the system actually use and do?"), reinforced in §22.4 with the correct assertion that neither is reconstructible from the other. §22.1 places runtime provenance outside rgkb on the correct grounds (minors' personal context, retention entanglement, access-boundary containment), consistent with §3.3. §22.3 constrains runtime records to reference RGKB only through immutable canonical version identifiers and forbids copying canonical content or writing into rgkb. §22.2 captures the full required set: rule versions, guardrail versions and their evaluation outcomes, KU versions, validation state, rights state, human-review event, and model/provider/system version. §9.5 and §21.5 fence canonical provenance and governance events off from student-specific records from the other direction. §22.5 correctly scopes the runtime store's own design out while keeping the boundary in scope.

The separation itself is sound and I found no leakage path. Two refinements are recorded as findings rather than closure failures: "validation state relied upon" has no immutable citable identity, which sits in tension with §22.3's identifiers-only rule (F-05); and the localized-text version is missing from §22.2 (F-02, shared with R-01).

R-03 Governance Bindings — CLOSED

§16.1 promotes all five required relationships to first-class typed entities bound to immutable version identities, with binding-level semantics (required vs advisory, role played). §4.2(23) and §16.2 remove free-text scope from the authoritative path, and §16.2 states the correct failure argument — silent guardrail detachment via renamed constructs or translated scope strings. §14.2 and §15.2 explicitly delete v0.1's free-text scope, evidence_basis, and construct_scope. §16.3 makes guardrail prohibition precedence explicit and unidirectional ("rules cannot raise themselves through the safety floor"). §16.4 states transitive fail-closed behaviour over invalid, superseded, unavailable, or gate-failing dependencies, and §14.3 forecloses the two dangerous readings ("proceed with a warning", "fall back to unconstrained model reasoning"). Dependencies are traversable and superseded dependencies are detectable via §17.2 axis 4.

The residual issue is not detection but responsibility: nothing assigns who must re-bind, and neither branch of the supersession decision is managed (F-04).

4. Architecture Preserved Correctly

Carry into Controlled Schema Specification without reopening:

1. Dedicated rgkb schema; public.knowledge_resources as governed projection only, never a back-door writer (§3.1–§3.2, §24.3).
2. Operational student data — including assessment results, responses, and platform-derived norms — permanently outside rgkb (§3.3, §10.5).
3. Stable identity / immutable version split with draft-only mutability and indefinite historical resolvability (§5.1–§5.3).
4. Prohibition of the mutable integer version column as a sole mechanism (§5.4).
5. Version chain ≠ supersession relation, with partial (scope-bounded) supersession as a required capability (§5.5, §11.3).
6. Semantics-free, immutable, never-reused, never-re-pointed, collision-safe domain codes; rejection of KU-001-CH5-014; three separate identifier families (§5.6).
7. Source / Expression / Manifestation three-level split; evidence binds scientifically at Expression, physically resolves at Manifestation; fingerprints prove file integrity, not edition identity (§6.1–§6.3).
8. Level-aware external identifiers rather than fixed-level DOI/ISBN/URL columns (§6.4).
9. Normalized rights model with explicit allow/deny, purpose, jurisdiction, conditions, validity window, revocation, typed evidence, immutable decision history — and fail-closed treatment of unknown, missing, expired, and revoked (§7.2–§7.5).
10. Rights-conditioned excerpt retention, with retention permission strictly separate from output/quotation permission, and the prohibition on parking restricted excerpts in notes (§7.6).
11. Typed evidence links as the sole authoritative pointer; free text may annotate only (§9.3, §4.2(24)).
12. A distinct rights/document anchor concept reachable through one coherent evidence-linking contract (§9.4).
13. The two-chain provenance separation and its boundary rules (§9.1, §22).
14. Instrument / instrument-version / scale registry with typed, evidence-linked, validation-governable construct↔scale mappings and explicit operationalizes / partially_operationalizes / proxy_for semantics (§10.3–§10.4).
15. Version-to-version relations; removal of relation-local validation_status and evidence_basis (§11.1–§11.2).
16. One immutable append-only decision/review substrate with derived current state; mandatory reviewer attribution including role, authority, and conflict-of-interest (§12.1–§12.3).
17. Reviewer identity independent of auth.users; no fabricated accounts for external experts (§12.4).
18. Referential enforcement for governance subjects, with polymorphism permitted only for append-only audit (§12.6).
19. Claim/KU-version-level cross-source comparison with first-class participating positions; rejection of outcome-plus-confidence-scalar (§13.1–§13.3).
20. Executable specification requirement: named + versioned + validated specification language, validity as an activation gate, and fail-closed on unparseable/unevaluable content with no fallback to unconstrained generation (§14.3–§14.4, §15.3).
21. Structured human-review policy (type, required authority, blocking vs advisory, unavailable-review behaviour) (§15.4).
22. Five first-class typed bindings, guardrail-prohibition precedence, transitive fail-closed dependency behaviour (§16).
23. Four independent lifecycle axes; approval derived from review events; no self-granted approval; governed reactivation after quarantine (§17).
24. Language-declaring governed text; translation_fidelity distinct from georgian_context; no inherited validation; no ad-hoc generation-time authoritative translation (§18).
25. Seven separately authorized curation acts; asserter ≠ approver; ad-hoc SQL not a normal interface; exceptional administration auditable and non-exempt (§19).
26. The full security stack: schema non-exposure as a drift-controlled verifiable property, zero direct grants to anon/authenticated, hardened bridge functions owned by a dedicated least-privilege role, and service-role possession ≠ authorization (§20).
27. Append-only actor-attributed governance events with actor types beyond auth.users (§21).
28. Citations reframed as renderings of structured metadata, generated with overrides (§24).
29. Retrieval deferral preserving version identity, span offsets, and retention state; future embeddings must bind to immutable versions (§25).

Finding: §10.2 justifies the registry on enforceability grounds — a guardrail is only enforceable if the architecture "can name the actual scale the runtime sees" — but v0.2 never defines the correspondence between rgkb.instrument_scale and the operational scoring channel, and assigns that correspondence to neither the canonical nor the operational domain. F-04
Severity: HIGH
Section/entity: §16.1, §16.4, §17.2 axis 4
Finding: Bindings reference immutable version identities and §16.4 fails closed on superseded dependencies, but no actor, event, or review obligation is assigned for re-binding when a new guardrail or KU version is issued. Both branches are unmanaged: marking the old version superseded hard-fails every dependent rule at once, while not marking it leaves rules bound to knowledge the curators have already replaced.
Why it matters: Because rule versions and their bindings are immutable, re-binding implies minting a new version of every dependent rule — an unacknowledged cascade. The predictable operational response is to avoid marking supersession, which converts a safety property into silent under-governance.
Failure mode if ignored: Either a mass fail-closed outage of student-facing interpretation on a routine guardrail clarification, or a standing practice of never superseding, leaving consequential rules bound to knowledge the organisation no longer stands behind.
Minimal correction: State whether bindings carry their own lifecycle, and assign the detection and re-binding responsibility (a governance event plus a review obligation) for dependent objects when a bound version is superseded.

F-05
Severity: HIGH

Finding: §22.2 requires the runtime record to capture "the validation state relied upon at that time" and "the rights state relied upon at that time". 9. Readiness for Controlled Schema Specification

NO — not yet, by a narrow and additive margin.

The model is conceptually sound, its boundaries are correct, and R-02 and R-03 are closed. But three items determine which conceptual entities exist and where a boundary falls, and per the stated classification rule those must be settled before entering the schema-spec stage rather than during it.

Exact remaining preconditions:

1. F-01 — Enumerate which governed objects take the §5.1 identity/version split beyond the current five, and state immutability-on-reference for evidence anchors (correction creates a new anchor). Required because §12.3's mandatory "exact subject VERSION reviewed" is currently unsatisfiable for subjects §10.4 and §11.2 declare validation-governable.
2. F-02 — Bind localized governed text to a version identity in §5.1 and §5.3, change §23.1's "localization identity" to localized-text version identity, and add the localized-text version(s) used to §22.2's runtime record contents.
3. F-03 — State that a governed correspondence exists between canonical scale identity and the operational scoring channel, and allocate its ownership to one domain.

These are three additive clarifications consistent with invariants v0.2 already states (§4.2(21), §4.2(23), §5.2). They reopen no settled decision, require no redesign, move no domain boundary already drawn, and should not require re-review of the whole model — a v0.2.1 amendment with confirmation limited to these three points is sufficient.

Findings F-04 through F-14 are correctly resolvable within Controlled Schema Specification and are not preconditions.
