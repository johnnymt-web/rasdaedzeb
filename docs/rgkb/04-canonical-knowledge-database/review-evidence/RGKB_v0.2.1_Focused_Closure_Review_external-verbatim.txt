3. F-01 Closure

CLOSED

Pattern A / Pattern B. The distinction is coherent and correctly motivated. Pattern A (§5.7.1) is scoped to enduring concepts carrying successive semantic revisions; Pattern B (§5.7.2) to atomic historical assertions, locations, relations, positions, and events whose correction is "a different thing," not "a revision of the same thing." Neither permits in-place mutation of historical governed meaning: §5.7.3 defines the boundary as draft-exit for A and first-governance-use for B, with five enumerated triggers. Critically, §5.7.2/§5.7.5 explicitly refuse to manufacture identity/version families on atomic records — satisfying F-01A(5) and removing the artificial-version-family pressure the v0.2 rule created.

I probed the one window Pattern B leaves open: an anchor edited while referenced only by a draft KU version. At publication the KU exits draft, the anchor becomes "referenced by a non-draft governed version," and immutability attaches. No governance-bearing determination existed during the mutable window. The boundary holds.

Evidence anchors (F-01B). §9.2.1 freezes every field the review named — source-expression reference, manifestation reference, locator type and payload, page/section/paragraph/span coordinates, character and span offsets, anchor_text_hash, and retained excerpt identity and content. Correction is explicitly new anchor, never update in place, with historical references continuing to resolve to the original. §9.4 extends the same semantics to rights/document anchors.

Original failure-mode test: can a past KU version or runtime trace resolve through the same anchor identifier to different evidence than was reviewed? No. The identifier is immutable, its locator payload is immutable, and the excerpt is hash-pinned.

Construct↔scale mapping (F-01C). §10.4.1 requires immutability after governance use, permitting either realization, with the architectural requirement stated as immutability plus exact historical identity and the table shape deferred to §26.6. Correct level of abstraction.

KU relations. §11.1.1 freezes predicate, source KU version, target KU version, scope qualification, and evidence linkage after governance use. The rationale is exactly right: a reviewer who adjudicated a contradiction must see the contradiction as stated at adjudication time.

Cross-source positions. §13.2.1 freezes KU/source reference, stance, qualifiers, and evidence binding once included in an adjudication, and states that historical adjudications must remain reconstructable against the original positions.

Validation subject identity (F-01D). §12.3.1 replaces "exact subject VERSION" with "exact IMMUTABLE GOVERNED SUBJECT INSTANCE" — version identifier for A, record identifier for B — and §12.6 requires the referential-enforcement technique to reference both instance kinds. This resolves the v0.2 inconsistency without fake version families, which was the precise defect.

One residual coverage gap is recorded as M-1 below. It does not reopen F-01's conceptual machinery: §5.7 states the universal rule that every governance-bearing object must be assigned to exactly one pattern, so the gap is in the illustrative enumeration, not in the semantics.

4. F-02 Closure

CLOSED

I tested all fourteen required properties against §18.6 and its cross-references. All fourteen hold:

Stable localization identity is permitted "where appropriate" (1) — correctly matching the review's "may," since the mandatory half is the version identity. Each governed translation/adaptation is an immutable localized-text version (2). Draft editing is allowed only before the boundary (3). Immutability attaches on any of exit-from-draft, review, validation, binding, activation, publication, or runtime use (4) — the runtime-use trigger is the one that actually closes the student-facing hole. Correction requires a new version, with the explicit statement that "the wording a student saw is never rewritten under them" (5). Historical versions remain resolvable indefinitely, including superseded, retracted, and quarantined (6). translation_fidelity attaches to the exact localized-text version, not to the localization identity and not to the source-language version (7, §12.5 amended to match). georgian_context remains a distinct dimension with non-inheritance stated in both directions (8, 9, 10). §22.2 requires consequential runtime provenance to capture the exact localized-text version(s) actually rendered or supplied (11). §23.1 requires traceability to resolve version identity, not merely localization identity (12). §18.6.1 states the guarantee in terms (13): "Knowing the Knowledge Unit version is NOT sufficient to prove what Georgian wording a student saw." §18.6.2 forbids ephemeral generation-time translation from becoming canonical, and adds that generated text "never becomes a localized-text version by having been shown" (14).

Original failure-mode test: can the Georgian wording change after a report is issued while the historic runtime trace still points to the same governed localization identity? No. The trace records a version, not an identity (§22.2); that version is immutable once used at runtime (§18.6(4)); correction mints a new version (§18.6(5)); and the old version stays resolvable (§18.6(6)).

The domain reasoning in §18.6.1 is substantively correct, not decorative: two Georgian renderings of the same KU version can differ in developmental register in ways that change what a 14-year-old understands about their own future. Traceability that stopped at the KU version could not answer a parent's or counselor's question. That is the right justification for the requirement.

5. F-03 Closure

CLOSED

Domain ownership (F-03A). §10.7.1–§10.7.2 keep canonical instrument identity, instrument-version identity, scale identity, and construct↔scale semantics in RGKB, and place execution identity, scoring-channel/version identity, and the correspondence itself in the operational domain. The stated reason is the correct one: operational identifiers change for engineering reasons carrying no scientific meaning, and RGKB must not absorb unstable implementation strings as canonical scientific identity. §10.7.2 explicitly denies that canonical identifiers denote any administration, session, submission, or scoring run.

Authoritative correspondence (F-03B). §10.7.3 requires the seam to be explicit, auditable, version-aware, testable, and fail-closed — all five named. It prohibits free-text aliases, assessment_type strings, function names, question-set labels, and naming conventions from constituting the authoritative mapping, permitting them only as migration or lookup inputs, with resolution through stable identities. The tie to §16.2 is apt: a correspondence resting on a string can detach silently.

I verified this premise against the repository read-only rather than accepting it on the document's word. assessment_type is live across 20+ tracked files including supabase/functions/submit-assessment/, and src/utils/assessmentNormalization.ts carries two divergent string vocabularies in one file — type: "riasec" | ... | "bigfive" | "workvalues" alongside source: "riasec" | ... | "big_five" | "work_values". That is exactly the silent-detachment class §10.7.3 prohibits, and the operational side today has no scoring-channel version identity at all. v0.2.1 does not assume one exists; it requires one be established and fails closed until it is. The seam is compatible with the codebase and materially necessary to it.

Change safety (F-03C). §10.7.4 requires explicit review and, where required, re-establishment on change to scoring implementation, question set, scoring logic, channel naming, instrument version, or canonical scale supersession, with "no silent inheritance across scientifically meaningful version changes" stated flatly.

Fail-closed (F-03D). §10.7.4 requires the consequential path to fail closed on unresolvable correspondence, and explicitly forbids guessing from strings, falling back to a default channel, and proceeding with an unbound interpretation. Reinforced at §14.3 (guardrail blocks the dependent consequential path) and §15.3 (same for interpretation rules), so the requirement is load-bearing in two places, not stated once.

Student-data boundary (F-03E). §10.7.5 excludes all six named categories from RGKB and characterizes the correspondence as a boundary artefact naming a canonical identifier on one side and an operational channel identity on the other, never carrying a student-linked value across in either direction. §10.5 and §3.3 are intact.

Residual: fail-closed protects against a missing correspondence but not against a present but wrong one, and the correspondence lives outside §12's reviewer-authority domain and §21's canonical audit events. Recorded as M-2. All five tested F-03 criteria are literally satisfied; reviewer authority was not among them, so this tracks rather than blocks.

Related closure: F-01
Finding: The §5.7.4 coverage-assignment table omits the source hierarchy (rgkb.source, rgkb.source_expression, rgkb.source_manifestation) and rgkb.external_identifier, while §12.5 retains source_identity as a validation dimension and §12.3.1/§4.2.1(36) require every validated subject to be cited by an exact immutable governed instance identity.
Why it matters: §6.3 states that edition identity is "a curated, evidence-backed determination, subject to the source_identity validation dimension." That makes the source expression a validated governance subject, so §12.3.1 demands it have an immutable instance identity — but the table that assigns instance identity does not list it.
Failure mode if ignored: Controlled Schema Specification realizes source_expression as an in-place mutable record. A source_identity validation determination then cites a subject whose edition designation can later be silently corrected, and the determination no longer describes what was validated. The universal rule in §5.7 ("each governance-bearing object must be assigned to exactly one of them") already covers them semantically; only the enumeration is short.
